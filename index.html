Every speaker in the room, playing together.

Windows sends sound to one output at a time. Audio Mirror plays it through up to six — a Bluetooth speaker, a TV over HDMI, the desk speakers — and holds them in step instead of letting them drift apart.

The signal path

Where the sound actually goes

Four stages, in the order the audio travels. The channel names are the ones Windows itself uses.

01 — capture

Take the sound

Reads what your apps are already playing, using the standard Windows audio interfaces. Nothing is recorded and nothing leaves the PC.

02 — bus

Build the channels

Optionally derives a centre, a sub and two rears from ordinary stereo, with a crossover you set between 40 and 250 Hz.

FLFRC LFEBLBR
03 — route

Give each speaker a job

Front left here, rear right there, or the whole mix everywhere. One channel sent to a stereo speaker plays on both of its sides.

04 — align

Line them up

Bluetooth runs behind wired by 150–250 ms. A per-speaker delay closes the gap, and a drift controller keeps it closed.

What it does

Built for a real room, not a spec sheet

Six outputs

Bluetooth, HDMI, USB, optical — any combination of outputs Windows can see, each with its own volume.

Delay that stays put

Every sound card runs off its own crystal, so speakers slide apart over minutes. A controller trims the resampling continuously to stop that.

Surround from stereo

A matrix derives centre, sub and rear channels from ordinary two-channel audio, so any music gets a surround layout — not just films.

Balance a linked pair

Two speakers joined to each other arrive as one device. Level each side and hold back whichever sits closer — about 3 ms per metre.

Sound profiles

Movie, Concert, Games, Late night and more. Each sets tone and surround together, because a film and a podcast want different rooms.

Take over every speaker

Capture other apps rather than tapping a speaker, and the one Windows plays to becomes an output like any other.

Privacy

Nothing it hears ever leaves your PC

LAST UPDATED 6 SEPTEMBER 2026

The short version

Audio Mirror collects nothing. It contains no network code at all.

What it does with audio

To play your sound on more than one speaker, Audio Mirror reads the audio your PC is already playing, using the standard Windows audio interfaces, and writes it back out to the speakers you chose. That audio is held in memory for the fraction of a second it takes to pass along, then discarded. It is never written to disk, never transmitted, and never shown to anyone. The app cannot record.

What is stored on your PC

Two files, both on your own machine and neither ever transmitted: your settings — which speakers you picked, their delay, volume, routing and appearance — and an error log, written only if the app hits a problem, containing the error and the app version. No audio, no personal information. Uninstalling removes them.

What is not collected

No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting to us, no advertising identifiers, no account, no sign-in, no personal information of any kind. The app makes no outbound connections, so there is nowhere for anything to go.

Permissions

Audio Mirror asks Windows for access to your audio devices so it can play sound through them. It uses no camera, no location, no contacts, and no files outside its own settings.

Children

Audio Mirror is a general-purpose utility, collects nothing, and is suitable for all ages.
